AI protein design

Find a binder. Or design one for the job.

Phage display searches a library of antibody fragments carried by tiny viruses. AI design starts with the target and proposes proteins shaped to fit it. Both use lab tests to find what works.

Phage display vs AI design

What to comparePhage displayAI design
Library9 billionFragments in a reusable library95Designs tested per target
Binder size25–27 kDaSingle-chain antibody fragment (scFv)4–10 kDaFusiomica’s miniprotein range
AffinityLow nMFragments against N-cadherinAbout 30 nMBinders against HA and IL-7Rα
Main costsLibrary access or construction, selection rounds and assaysCompute, DNA synthesis, protein production and assays
Best forSearching a large, reusable libraryChoosing a binding site, size or geometry up front
Trade-offYou select from what the library containsSuccess depends on the target model and lab screen

Published examples use different targets and assays. Lower nM means tighter binding.

What changes with AI?

Design can start from a target model before purified protein is available. That can save time and lets you try jobs such as bridging two proteins. The lab checks whether the design binds, what it avoids and what it does.
