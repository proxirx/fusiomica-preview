One cancer marker, two ways to use it

We’re developing one binder for a marker found across cancer types. It could help us locate tumours and deliver treatment to the same target.

What is theranostics?

Theranostics pairs imaging and treatment around the same target. Our approach starts with one protein binder: attach an imaging label to see where it goes, or a therapeutic radionuclide to deliver radiation to those sites.

Lead optimisation and imaging in cancer cells

We’re developing a binder for a marker found across cancer types. We measure its activity as we optimise the lead, then label it with fluorescence to see how it binds to cancer cells.

Phase 0: measure where the binder goes

  1. Give a microdose
  2. Scan with PET
  3. Map uptake

A first-in-human PET study uses a microdose of the radiolabelled binder. The endpoint is imaging: how much tracer reaches tumour lesions, where else it goes and how long it stays. Eligible microdose tracers can enter the clinic with a narrower scope of manufacturing and controls (CMC) and a smaller nonclinical safety package. Clinical-grade production, quality controls and dosimetry remain part of the work.

FDA guidance on microdose radiopharmaceuticals

Test less explored targets in people sooner

Eroom’s law describes a long decline in drug R&D productivity. Historically, approvals per billion dollars of research spending, adjusted for inflation, roughly halved every nine years. We want to shorten the time it takes to get an answer in people.

We aim to get to a small, supervised Phase 0 PET study as soon as the safety work and approvals allow. Animal studies help us plan that study. PET then shows where the tracer actually goes in people. We use those data to decide whether to advance, redesign or stop.

How ADCs and radioligands deliver treatment

A radionuclide decays at a known rate. By measuring uptake and retention, we can estimate the radiation dose to tumours and healthy organs. Treatment response is a separate clinical question.

EANM guidance on dosimetry · FDA guidance on ADCs

Too many programmes chase the same targets

A less explored target can look too risky to justify a large trial. A small PET study can first show whether the binder reaches human tumours and where else it accumulates. That narrows the delivery risk and gives us evidence to decide whether a larger investment is warranted.
